Glossary & Explanation

This section explains all terms used in the comparison table above. Each term is defined in a neutral, functional way to support clear comparison.

ART TYPES

Identity & Branding
Visual systems designed for long-term recognition and consistency. Includes logos, wordmarks, and brand systems.

Illusion & Perception
Visual constructions that manipulate perception, cognition, or interpretation using optical or conceptual techniques.

Communication & Marketing
Visual outputs created to persuade, promote, or trigger short-term action.

Information & Instruction
Designs focused on clarity, accuracy, and understanding. Emotion is minimized in favor of precision.

Editorial & Content
Narrative-driven layouts that organize reading, storytelling, or thematic presentation.

Digital & Interface
Functional visuals used inside apps, websites, or systems where usability is critical.

Art & Expression
Emotion-led or idea-led visuals created primarily for expression rather than utility.

Packaging & Physical
Graphics applied to physical objects to influence purchase, recognition, or use.

Corporate & Formal
Structured visuals designed to convey authority, compliance, and professionalism.

Psychological & Symbolic
Visual systems that operate at cultural, symbolic, or belief-based levels.

CHARACTERISTICS

Action orientation
Whether the output is designed to trigger an immediate user action.

Emotional intensity
The degree to which emotion is intentionally used to influence perception.

Longevity
Whether the output is intended for long-term use.

Memory impact
Whether the output is designed to be remembered after exposure.

Precision requirement
The level of accuracy required for the output to function correctly.

Scalability requirement
Whether the output must perform consistently across sizes, platforms, or contexts.

Storytelling emphasis
Whether narrative structure is a core component.

Cultural / legal relevance
Whether cultural norms, symbolism, or legal constraints are significant factors.

YES / NO VALUES

Yes
The characteristic is fundamental to that art type.

No
The characteristic is not essential and may be absent without issue.

This glossary ensures the comparison table can be read consistently without subjective interpretation.
